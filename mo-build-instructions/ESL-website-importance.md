ESL's website is strategically important to its business model—not simply as a marketing channel, but as a way to acquire members, deepen financial relationships, reduce operating costs, and build trust.

For a credit union like ESL, I would argue that the website is increasingly part of the core business infrastructure, even though the actual financial transactions may be handled by separate banking platforms.

The distinction is important: ESL doesn't make money because people visit its website. It makes money when those visits lead to funded accounts, profitable lending relationships, retained deposits, and broader financial relationships.

## 4. Where I see the biggest opportunity

From a digital experience and AI strategy perspective, I would focus on the shift from product navigation to customer intent.

For example, a visitor might arrive at ESL.org thinking:

- "I'm buying my first home. How much can I afford?"
- "I have $50,000 sitting in checking. What should I do with it?"
- "I'm starting a business and need financing."
- "Should I refinance my auto loan?"

Traditionally, the visitor navigates menus, compares products, reads content, and eventually finds an application or contacts someone.

An intent-driven experience could understand the visitor's goal, ask appropriate questions, explain relevant options, and guide them toward the right product or a qualified ESL representative.

In financial services, that would require strong controls around privacy, accuracy, disclosures, eligibility, and the distinction between education and regulated financial advice.

## 5. How I would measure website success

I would avoid evaluating ESL's website primarily through page views, engagement, or even raw conversion rates.

Instead, I would connect digital experience metrics to financial outcomes.

| Website KPI                              | Business outcome               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| New funded accounts                      | Deposit growth                 |
| Funded loan volume from digital journeys | Interest-earning asset growth  |
| Cost per acquired member                 | Acquisition efficiency         |
| Products per member                      | Relationship depth             |
| Digital self-service completion          | Lower servicing expense        |
| Application abandonment                  | Lost acquisition opportunities |
| Member satisfaction and retention        | Long-term relationship value   |

An illustrative example: if improvements to the website generated 1,000 additional funded loans annually, averaging $25,000 each, that would represent $25 million in additional loan originations. The actual earnings impact would depend on loan yield, funding costs, credit losses, expenses, and whether that lending was incremental.

## My assessment

For ESL, the website should be treated as a strategic digital distribution and relationship channel, not merely a content management platform.

Its importance extends across marketing, lending, deposits, member service, and wealth management.

The question I would ask ESL's leadership is:

"How much of your member growth, deposit acquisition, loan origination, and relationship expansion is influenced by ESL.org—and can you measure that influence across the entire member journey?"

If they cannot answer that question, there may be a substantial opportunity to connect their digital experience strategy more directly to their business model.

And that leads to an interesting distinction: modernizing ESL's website is a technology project; turning it into a measurable engine for member growth and relationship value is a business transformation initiative.
